Streaming the golden age of early cinema: 1900s–1910s

Fantasmagorie (1908)

The world's first fully animated film by Émile Cohl.

Released in 1908, Fantasmagorie is widely considered by film historians to be the world's first fully animated film. Created by French caricaturist and animator Émile Cohl, each frame was drawn on paper and shot sequentially onto negative film, giving it the distinct appearance of chalk lines drawn on a chalkboard.

"A Trip to the Moon" (1902)

Georges Méliès' landmark sci-fi masterpiece.

Released in 1902, A Trip to the Moon is widely regarded as the earliest science fiction film. Directed by Georges Méliès, it charmed audiences with innovative special effects, trick photography, and imaginative set designs.

"The Great Train Robbery" (1903)

Edwin S. Porter's pioneering Western action film.

Directed by Edwin S. Porter in 1903, this landmark silent film established the narrative blueprint for the American Western genre, featuring dynamic camera movement and cross-cutting action sequences.

"The Perils of Pauline" - Episode 1 (1914)

The classic cliffhanger serial starring Pearl White.

Released in 1914, this legendary action serial cemented the enduring cultural trope of the endangered heroine, starring Pearl White as the adventurous Pauline facing relentless weekly perils.

"Regeneration" (1915)

Raoul Walsh's gritty landmark gangster drama.

Directed by Raoul Walsh in 1915, Regeneration is considered by film historians to be one of the earliest feature-length gangster films, celebrated for its gritty, authentic location shooting on the streets of New York City.

"Intolerance" (1916)

D.W. Griffith's monumental historical epic.

Released in 1916, Intolerance remains a marvel of silent cinema scale, featuring colossal Babylonian sets and an ambitious structure that intertwines four separate historical narratives across millennia.

"The Immigrant" (1917)

Charlie Chaplin's classic comedy short.

Created in 1917, The Immigrant showcases Charlie Chaplin's iconic Tramp character arriving in the United States, brilliantly blending masterclass physical comedy with sincere social commentary.

"The Portage Trail" (c. 1915)

A silent melodrama featuring early 20th-century Akron, Ohio.

This silent film tells the story of a romance between a minister's son and a banker's daughter against the backdrop of Akron, Ohio, serving as a historic time capsule for local water infrastructure development.
